# Dutch Door: market research

Research date: 2026-11-10. "Not found" means no credible published figure turned up. Weak sources are flagged.

## 1. Market size

- **Brokerage firms.** IBISWorld counts 3,237 business-broker businesses in the US in 2025, up 1.5% from 2024 [[3]](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/business-brokers/4796/).
- **Individual brokers.** Not found. No primary source gives a headcount.
- **IBBA membership.** IBBA reports over 3,239 members, its largest ever, and close to 500 Certified Business Intermediaries (March 2026) [[4]](https://www.ibba.org/articles/building-the-future-of-our-profession-together/). IBBA has non-US members, so this is not a US count.
- **Annual sales.** BizBuySell recorded 9,586 closed sales in 2025, up 0.4%, with $7.95 billion in total enterprise value [[2]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/). This covers deals reported to BizBuySell, not every US sale.
- **Latest quarter.** Q3 2026 had 2,132 closed deals, down 7% year over year [[1]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/). Service businesses made up 992 of them (47%) [[1]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/).
- **Median sale price.** $350,000 in both 2025 [[2]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/) and Q3 2026 [[1]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/). The $1M to $5M target sits well above the median. The share of deals in that band: not found.
- **Time to close.** Median 170 days in 2025 [[2]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/).
- **Commission.** For sales up to $1 million, 8% to 15%, with 10% most common. Larger deals often use the Lehman scale: 5% of the first $1 million, then 4%, 3%, 2%, and 1% above $4 million [[5]](https://www.bizquest.com/learn/article/broker-and-ma-advisory-fees). An exact rate for $1M to $5M deals: not found.

## 2. The inquiry problem

Hard data is thin. No source measures the problem.

- **Inquiries per listing.** No measured figure found. BizBuySell says brokers get "dozens of inquiries" per quality listing [[6]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/business-broker-inquiry-best-practices/).
- **Share unqualified.** Not found in any primary source. BizBuySell says only that "the majority of ad respondents never make a purchase" [[7]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/how-to-identify-qualified-buyers-for-your-business/).
- **Vendor claims (unverified, weak).** A firm that sells agent builds claims 8% of inquiries reach an NDA, and that a 4-broker firm gets 200 to 600 inquiries a month and spends 50 to 90 hours a month on triage [[9]](https://openclawconsult.com/lab/openclaw-business-brokers). It cites no data.
- **Time spent screening.** Not found in any primary source.
- **Who is inquiring.** 54% of surveyed buyers have never owned a business [[39]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/whos-buying-small-businesses-q2-2026/). Meanwhile 31% of buyers say finding a quality business is their main obstacle [[1]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/).
- **IBBA Market Pulse.** The full survey results are password protected [[40]](https://www.ibba.org/market-pulse-q1-2026-survey-results/). Ask an IBBA member.

**Standard qualification steps:**

1. Buyer inquires on a blind listing or teaser.
2. Buyer signs an NDA and completes a buyer profile, financial statement, and disclosure statement [[8]](https://morganandwestfield.com/knowledge/process-for-screening-buyers-when-selling-a-business/).
3. Broker screens for experience, cash at closing, timeline, and loan eligibility [[7]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/how-to-identify-qualified-buyers-for-your-business/).
4. Buyer shows proof of funds, an SBA pre-qualification letter, or investor backing [[6]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/business-broker-inquiry-best-practices/).
5. Broker releases the CIM, then more documents or a seller meeting, then an LOI [[8]](https://morganandwestfield.com/knowledge/process-for-screening-buyers-when-selling-a-business/).

**Agent standards, November 2026:**

- **MCP (agent to tools). Adopted.** Hosted by the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), which has 305 members [[23]](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/agentic-ai-foundation-surpasses-300-members-in-less-than-one-year). A July 2026 update aligned its authorization with OAuth 2.0 [[24]](https://www.enterpriseaiworld.com/Articles/News/News/The-Agentic-AI-Foundation-Updates-Core-Features-of-the-Model-Context-Protocol-175887.aspx).
- **A2A (agent to agent). Adopted.** Version 1.0 is stable, with more than 150 supporting organizations and Signed Agent Cards for identity [[21]](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year). It joined AAIF on August 17, 2026 [[22]](https://aaif.io/blog/a2a-joins-aaif).
- **AP2 (agent payments). Proposed.** Google gave it to the FIDO Alliance in April 2026. No finished specification [[25]](https://fidoalliance.org/fido-alliance-to-develop-standards-for-trusted-ai-agent-interactions/).
- **Web Bot Auth (agent request signing). Draft, but deployed.** An IETF Internet-Draft, version 01, dated October 29, 2026 [[26]](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol). Cloudflare already verifies signed agents, including ChatGPT agent [[27]](https://blog.cloudflare.com/zh-tw/signed-agents).
- **Visa Trusted Agent Protocol. Launched** October 2025, per a trade news site. A card-network scheme, not an open standard [[28]](https://forkast.news/know-your-agent-how-the-payments-industry-is-building-the-trust-layer-oauth-and-saml-couldnt/).
- **"Know Your Agent" vendors. Early.** Skyfire sells KYA tokens (vendor marketing; live status unstated) [[29]](https://skyfire.xyz/how-ai-agents-prove-identity-kya-authentication-authorization-payments/). Experian announced Agent Trust on April 30, 2026, described as "being developed" [[30]](https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/experian-announces-agent-trust-to-power-trusted-ai-driven-commer). No KYA standard exists [[28]](https://forkast.news/know-your-agent-how-the-payments-industry-is-building-the-trust-layer-oauth-and-saml-couldnt/).

The identity work targets payments and web access, not negotiation.

## 4. Legal notes (not legal advice)

**Can a software agent form a contract or sign an NDA?**

- **UETA Section 14** says electronic agents can form a contract "even if no individual was aware of or reviewed" their actions (Illinois text) [[32]](https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081503330K14.htm). UETA is law in 49 states plus D.C. New York uses its own statute [[33]](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/publications/client-alert-when-ai-agents-do-the-transaction-managing-contract-and-payment-risk/).
- **ESIGN, 15 U.S.C. 7001(h)**, protects contracts made by electronic agents when the action is "legally attributable to the person to be bound" [[31]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap96-subchapI-sec7001.htm). "Electronic agent" is defined at 15 U.S.C. 7006(3) (text not retrieved).
- So an agent-signed NDA can bind the principal in principle. The open question is attribution. Quinn Emanuel reports "no reported U.S. decision resolving that dispute" for generative agents [[33]](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/publications/client-alert-when-ai-agents-do-the-transaction-managing-contract-and-payment-risk/).
- UETA's error rule may let a person undo a deal if the agent gave no way to correct mistakes. Contracts cannot waive it [[34]](https://law.stanford.edu/2025/01/21/from-fine-print-to-machine-code-how-ai-agents-are-rewriting-the-rules-of-engagement-2).

**Could the software need a broker license?** A real risk. Get counsel before launch.

- BizBuySell lists 16 states that require a real estate license for business brokers: AK, AZ, CA, CO, FL, GA, ID, MN, NE, NV, OR, SD, UT, WA, WI, WY. Illinois requires registration instead [[35]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/business-broker-licenses-certifications/). Another source lists Michigan, not Washington [[36]](https://www.websiteclosers.com/resources/does-it-really-matter-that-business-brokers-are-not-licensed/).
- Sources do not rank strictness. Three states stand out:
  - **Illinois.** Its act covers anyone who, for pay, "assists or directs in the procuring of prospects" [[37]](https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081503070K10-5.10.htm). Exclusions not reviewed.
  - **California.** Soliciting buyers for, or negotiating, a business opportunity is licensed activity (B&P Code 10131). Online advertising that reaches residents counts as soliciting [[38]](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/faqs/FAQ_DoingBusinessOnTheInternet.pdf).
  - **Nevada.** Requires a business broker permit on top of a real estate license [[35]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/business-broker-licenses-certifications/).
- Risk rises if Dutch Door negotiates or takes pay tied to a closing. A flat software fee paid by a licensed broker is the safer shape.

**CAN-SPAM for one-to-one B2B cold email.** The FTC says "The law makes no exception for business-to-business email." Required: accurate headers, an honest subject line, ad disclosure, a postal address, and a clear opt-out honored within 10 business days. Penalties reach $53,088 per email [[41]](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business?level=0).

## 5. Pricing comparables

What brokers and buyers pay today:

- BusinessBroker.net membership: $44.95 a month (up to 50 listings) to $209.95 a month (151 to 200 listings, featured) [[17]](https://www.businessbroker.net/services/broker_price_schedule.aspx).
- BizBuySell single listings: $74.95, $99.95, or $199.95 a month on a 6-month term [[16]](https://www.bizbuysell.com/create-a-listing-FAQ/). Broker membership price: not found.
- IBBA dues: $475 a year individual; $675 corporate lead member [[18]](https://ibba.org/membership/join-the-ibba).
- General CRM: about $14 to $99 per user per month [[19]](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/best-crm-for-business-brokers).
- Managed inquiry handling (BizBuyReply): $300 a month plus $1,500 setup [[10]](https://bizbuyreply.pages.dev/).
- Buyers pay too: BizBuySell Edge is "as low as $20/month" [[20]](https://bizbuysell.com/Membership/BizBuySellEdge).
- Value anchor: 10% on a $1 million sale is $100,000 (arithmetic from [[5]](https://www.bizquest.com/learn/article/broker-and-ma-advisory-fees)).

**Hypothesis A (proposed, untested): flat per-broker subscription.** About $199 per broker per month, all listings included. That sits above CRM fees and below managed inquiry handling. Flat fees carry the least licensing risk.

**Hypothesis B (proposed, untested): per-listing fee plus a flat arbiter fee.** About $99 per active listing per month, plus about $250 per blind-match run, owed whether or not a deal is possible. Never tie fees to a closing.

Both prices are guesses to test in broker interviews. Neither comes from a source.
